# The Missing Cargo Ledger

Original supporting story outline for dunerealms.com. This is a narrative reference, not an announcement of implemented quests or new mechanics. Existing houses, planets, offices, items and commands retain their names and behavior.

## Characters

- **Tavren Sile**, an independent freight surveyor, checks cargo measurements against arrival receipts. His reputation rests on a shipment he once certified incorrectly; he wants to correct the record without concealing his own mistake.
- **Mira Vesk**, a water-route coordinator, protects the crews who depend on reliable deliveries. She distrusts Tavren's paperwork until his measurements explain shortages her crews have been blamed for.
- **Archivist Elian Voss**, a civilian records custodian on Kaiterhof, preserves conflicting shipment reports. Elian must decide whether to release evidence that also exposes negligence in the archive.

The three are unrelated adults. Their authority comes from their work and the evidence they can defend.

## Story sequence

A routine Arrakduin delivery arrives short of its recorded water allocation. Tavren finds that the seal numbers agree while the recorded container capacities do not. Mira traces the discrepancy through the delivery schedule and establishes which crews actually handled each shipment. Their initial disagreement becomes a partnership when both sets of records point to the same altered entry.

Elian locates two independently filed copies on Kaiterhof. One records an ordinary cargo transfer; the other silently redirects part of the allocation. Neither copy establishes guilt on its own. The group follows the chain of signatures rather than treating a house affiliation as proof.

At the confrontation, Tavren admits his earlier certification error, Mira presents the crew receipts, and Elian supplies the archive's dated copies. Publishing the evidence costs each of them professional protection. Their success restores a disputed allocation and opens a public accounting of the route; it does not settle the wider rivalry between the Great Houses.

## Continuity

Keep the conflict about logistics, evidence and responsibility. No character inherits supernatural authority, takes a dynastic spouse, undergoes a transformation into a ruler, or resolves the conflict through a predetermined future. Planet and faction references establish the existing setting without changing gameplay.
