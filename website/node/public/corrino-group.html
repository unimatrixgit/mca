THE FOUNDER

Shaddam Corrino IV

Before Corrino became a name above a post office or a treasury door, it was a name carried beneath the Golden Lion. Our chronicle begins during the reign of Shaddam Corrino IV: an Emperor who understood that a distant province could be lost through a missing dispatch as surely as through a defeated army.

In the founding charter of our tale, he brought couriers, trusted keepers of coin and navigators under one imperial compact. A message must arrive. A deposit must be accounted for. A route must remain open. The three promises became the inheritance of Corrino Group.

Three thousand years later, the throne has changed hands, borders have shifted and old palaces have become dust. The charter remains. Each reigning Emperor takes the ceremonial chair of the group; the ledgers and daily service remain in the hands of its appointed custodians.

This is an original DuneRealms alternate-history chronicle inspired by Dune (1992). The companies, charter and later chronology are invented for our setting.

SPOST · THE FIRST PROMISE

Neither distance nor silence.

Founded as the Cursus Imperialis, the first imperial couriers travelled with sealed cylinders beneath their cloaks, carrying orders between palace outposts and the spice ports. Their red route-mark meant that a dispatch had entered the protection of the charter.

During the scattered centuries, local postmasters kept the network alive even when their rulers ceased to speak. Letters crossed borders that armies could not. The old long-form name, SpicePost, eventually gave way to the familiar Spost. Its sweeping red arch remembers the promise of a route completed.

IMPERIAL POSTBANK · THE SECOND PROMISE

A ledger outlasts a throne.

The Aerarium Corrini began beside the earliest courier stations, where there stood locked chests where traders could leave coin rather than carry it through hostile country. Receipts bearing the imperial mark became the ancestors of the Postbank account.

Over millennia, the chests became vaults and the receipts became numbered ledgers. The bank survived succession crises by remembering what belonged to whom. Its crown recalls the founding charter; its postal horn remembers the humble station where a travelling merchant first entrusted it with a purse.

VAERKRING · THE THIRD PROMISE

The way between worlds.

The Collegium Navigatorum, the charter’s fellowship of navigators, inherited routes older than the compact itself. Under the name Vaerkring, the Routekeepers recorded safe passages, maintained departure halls and carried people and cargo between the scattered lights of the Imperium.

When the long years made familiar constellations foreign, their charts held the network together. The compass and orbit in their emblem honour that duty: every world is distant until someone keeps the way open. Today, Vaerkring carries the travel tradition of the group into the next imperial age.
