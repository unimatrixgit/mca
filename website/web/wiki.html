DUNEREALMS.COM / WORLD WIKI

A desert of our own

This wiki explains the difference between the source works that inspired earlier project material and the names and rules used on this website. It is a comparison, not a reproduction of the books or a claim of an official adaptation.

Why the game differs from the books

We deliberately use original public names and an original supporting storyline to reduce reliance on protected fictional expression and reduce confusion about licensing or endorsement. We keep the broad desert science-fiction atmosphere: scarce water, rival houses, dangerous travel and resource trading. The gameplay story does not aim to retell the novels. Separate commemorative banknote artwork includes named Dune characters as unofficial fan interpretations.

Copyright and trademark concerns are part of that editorial decision. They are not a claim that every individual name is copyrighted, or that changing names alone resolves every rights issue. Artwork, detailed fictional expression and branding require separate consideration. Gameplay also differs because it must work with Minecraft, multiplayer balance and the released plugins.

Why our names have a Dutch influence

We chose Dutch-inspired names as a nod to the linguistic texture of Dune. The books use Landsraad, a name with a familiar Dutch form: land and raad, meaning council. A linguistic study of science-fiction word-building discusses that Dutch raad connection. Related forms also occur in other Germanic languages, including Scandinavian languages; we are not claiming that the term, or the books as a whole, originated exclusively in Dutch.

That connection inspired our own approach: names that suggest desert trade, water stewardship, travel and imperial institutions through Dutch sounds and word-building. This is an aesthetic choice for our fan setting, not a claim that our invented houses, planets or organisations are translations of the novels or officially approved replacements.

We draw on Dutch language and word-building to give our desert worlds an original identity: ideas of travel, circles of cooperation, water stewardship and trade inform names such as Vaerkring, Welvaarders and Koopkring. House Aerdrecht, House Harkveen, House Korenholt, House Oordwijk and the Vrijduin continue that direction. Arrakduin combines the Arrak- root with duin; Kaladam keeps the Kala- sound and adds dam; Giedermark keeps Gied- with mark; Kaiterhof retains Kait- with hof; and Sigmadraken IV preserves Sigma and the numbered-world form while echoing Dutch draken. These are invented setting names, not claims of historical Dutch institutions or book-accurate translations.

A broad linguistic influence can inspire new work. Our aim is to create our own names and expression rather than reproduce the source works. Changing a name does not, by itself, clear copyright, trademark or other rights in artwork, stories, characters, branding or adaptations. Neither this explanation nor the unofficial-project disclaimer represents a licence or rights-holder approval. We retain Landsraad as the requested label for the imperial spice and water appointments; it is not presented as an original coinage.

Our own supporting story

Tavren Sile is a freight surveyor, Mira Vesk coordinates water deliveries, and Archivist Elian Voss preserves cargo records. Their dispute concerns a missing allocation and contradictory receipts. They are unrelated adults whose choices and evidence drive the story. This is an original supporting outline, not a claim that a playable questline has already shipped.

Website names and existing server commands

The public website labels have changed. Existing plugin filenames, world IDs, command arguments and saved data can still use older technical identifiers. Use commands exactly as shown in the field manual. A website rename does not rename a saved Minecraft world or change faction abilities. Older PDF downloads and plugin releases may also use earlier terminology.

Commemorative note artwork

The Vaerkring Reserve notes feature Minecraft-style fan interpretations of Stilgar, Aliah, Ghanima and Leto, alongside original character Mira Vesk. The 5,000-Dunemark victory scene is imagined game artwork, not a book-accurate event. These character editions reintroduce source-work references; Dutch-inspired names, an original imperial seal and a disclaimer do not provide a licence for those characters or adaptations. The artwork is decorative game-currency art with no cash value and is not offered here as licensed merchandise.
